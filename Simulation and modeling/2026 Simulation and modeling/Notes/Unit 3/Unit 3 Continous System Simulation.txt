------------------------------------------------------------
  WHY CONTINUOUS SYSTEM SIMULATION?
------------------------------------------------------------

    . In the real world, many systems change CONTINUOUSLY
      over time — not in sudden jumps.
  
    . Temperature of a room does not jump from 20C to 25C
      instantly — it rises gradually every second.
  
    . Water in a tank does not disappear in steps — it flows
      out smoothly as a continuous stream.
  
    . Population of a city does not increase by whole numbers
      at fixed moments — it grows continuously.
  
    . These systems cannot be modeled with discrete event
      simulation (which models things that happen at specific
      time points like customer arrivals).
  



  . CONTINUOUS SIMULATION models systems where:
          . State variables change SMOOTHLY over time
          . Change is described by RATES (derivatives)
          . Mathematics of DIFFERENTIAL EQUATIONS applies
      



  . KEY DIFFERENCE:
          .......................................................
          DISCRETE SYSTEM       CONTINUOUS SYSTEM
          .......................................................
          Events happen at      State changes constantly
          specific time points  at every instant of time
      
          Example: customer     Example: temperature
          arrives at 10:05 AM   rising from 20C to 25C
      
          State variable jumps  State variable changes
          suddenly              smoothly and gradually
      
          Modeled with event    Modeled with differential
          scheduling            equations
          .......................................................
      



------------------------------------------------------------
3.1  CONTINUOUS SYSTEM MODELS
------------------------------------------------------------

  . WHAT IS A CONTINUOUS SYSTEM MODEL?
  .....................................

        . A mathematical representation of a system whose
          state variables change continuously with time.
    
        . The model describes HOW FAST the system changes
          at every instant — not what the state is at
          each specific moment.
    
        . The rate of change is expressed as a DERIVATIVE.
    
        . Derivative notation:
          dX/dt = rate of change of variable X with respect to time t
    
        . The model is a set of differential equations
          that, when solved, give the complete behavior
          of the system over time.





  . TYPES OF CONTINUOUS SYSTEM MODELS:
  .....................................

    1. LUMPED PARAMETER MODELS
       . System is treated as a whole unit.
       . One set of variables describes entire system.
       . Ordinary Differential Equations (ODEs) used.
       . Example: temperature of a room as one value.

    2. DISTRIBUTED PARAMETER MODELS
       . System varies in SPACE as well as time.
       . Different parts of system have different values.
       . Partial Differential Equations (PDEs) used.
       . Example: temperature distribution across a wall
         — different at each point, changing over time.





  . COMMON CONTINUOUS SYSTEM EXAMPLES:
  .....................................

    . POPULATION GROWTH:
          dP/dt = r . P
          where P = population, r = growth rate
          Population grows continuously, not in jumps.

    . RADIOACTIVE DECAY:
          dN/dt = -lambda . N
          where N = number of atoms, lambda = decay constant
          Atoms decay continuously and randomly.

    . BANK ACCOUNT WITH INTEREST:
          dA/dt = r . A
          where A = account balance, r = interest rate per unit time
          Balance grows continuously with compound interest.

    . COOLING OF AN OBJECT:
          dT/dt = -k . (T - T_env)
          Newton's Law of Cooling.
          T = object temperature, T_env = environment temperature
          Object cools continuously toward room temperature.





  . DATA EXAMPLE — COOLING OF HOT TEA:
  .....................................

    . Scenario: A cup of tea at 90C is left in a room at 25C.
    . Newton's Law: dT/dt = -k(T - T_env)
    . k = 0.05 per minute (cooling constant)
    . T_env = 25C




    . Solution: T(t) = T_env + (T0 - T_env) . e^(-k.t)
                T(t) = 25 + (90 - 25) . e^(-0.05t)
                T(t) = 25 + 65 . e^(-0.05t)


            . Temperatures at various times:




              Time (min)    T(t) Calculation              Temp (C)
              ........................................................
              0             25 + 65 x e^(0)   = 25 + 65   90.0
              5             25 + 65 x e^(-0.25)= 25 + 50.7 75.7
              10            25 + 65 x e^(-0.5) = 25 + 39.4 64.4
              20            25 + 65 x e^(-1.0) = 25 + 23.9 48.9
              30            25 + 65 x e^(-1.5) = 25 + 14.5 39.5
              60            25 + 65 x e^(-3.0) = 25 + 3.2  28.2
              90            25 + 65 x e^(-4.5) = 25 + 0.7  25.7




            . ANALYSIS:
                  . Temperature drops RAPIDLY at first (65C drop in 60 min)
                  . Rate of cooling SLOWS as tea approaches room temp
                  . Tea never falls BELOW room temperature (25C)
                  . This S-shaped approach to equilibrium is typical of
                    all continuous exponential decay systems
                  . The continuous model captures this gradual behavior
                    perfectly — discrete model cannot do this

3.2  DIFFERENTIAL AND PARTIAL DIFFERENTIAL EQUATIONS
------------------------------------------------------------

  . WHAT IS A DIFFERENTIAL EQUATION?
  ...................................

        . An equation that relates a function to its DERIVATIVES.
        . It describes how a quantity CHANGES over time or space.
        . Solving it gives the actual value of the quantity at
          any time — not just the rate of change.
    
        . Form:    dY/dt = f(Y, t)
          This says: "the rate of change of Y depends on
          the current value of Y and time t."
    

  ....................................
  A. ORDINARY DIFFERENTIAL EQUATIONS (ODE)
  ....................................

    . Involve derivatives with respect to ONE variable only
      (usually time t).
    . Describe systems that change over time as a whole.

    . FIRST ORDER ODE (one derivative):
      dY/dt = f(Y, t)

      Example: Population growth
      dP/dt = 0.03 . P
      "Population grows at 3% of current population per year."

      Solution:
            P(t) = P0 . e^(0.03t)
            If P0 = 1000000 (1 million people):
      
            Year   t   P(t) Calculation              Population
            ......................................................
            2020   0   1,000,000 x e^(0)             1,000,000
            2021   1   1,000,000 x e^(0.03)          1,030,455
            2025   5   1,000,000 x e^(0.15)          1,161,834
            2030   10  1,000,000 x e^(0.30)          1,349,859
            2040   20  1,000,000 x e^(0.60)          1,822,119
            2050   30  1,000,000 x e^(0.90)          2,459,603
      
            ANALYSIS: Population doubles roughly every 23 years
            at 3% growth rate. Continuous model shows smooth growth
            unlike census data which appears as discrete jumps.
      

                . SECOND ORDER ODE (two derivatives):
                  d²Y/dt² = f(Y, dY/dt, t)
                  Example: motion of a spring-mass system
                  m . d²x/dt² + c . dx/dt + k . x = F(t)
                  where x = displacement, m = mass, c = damping, k = spring constant
            
                  Used in: mechanical vibrations, electrical circuits,
                  pendulum motion, vehicle suspension.


  ....................................
  B. PARTIAL DIFFERENTIAL EQUATIONS (PDE)
  ....................................
      
          . Involve derivatives with respect to MORE THAN ONE variable
            (usually both time t AND space x or y or z).
          . Describe systems where behavior varies in both space and time.
          . Much more complex to solve than ODEs.
      
          . HEAT EQUATION (most famous PDE):
            dT/dt = alpha . d²T/dx²
            where T = temperature, x = position along a rod,
            alpha = thermal diffusivity of material
      
            "How fast temperature changes at any point depends on
            how curved the temperature distribution is at that point."
      
          . SCENARIO — Heat along a metal rod:
            A 1-meter metal rod is heated at one end (left = 100C)
            and cooled at other end (right = 0C).
            Initial temperature = 0C everywhere except left end.
      
            Temperature distribution at different times:
      
                    Position x    t=0s    t=10s   t=30s   t=60s   Steady
                    (fraction     (C)     (C)     (C)     (C)     State (C)
                    of rod)
                    ..........................................................
                    0.0 (left)    100     100     100     100     100
                    0.1           0       18      42      68      90
                    0.2           0       8       24      48      80
                    0.3           0       4       14      35      70
                    0.4           0       2       8       24      60
                    0.5 (middle)  0       1       4       16      50
                    0.6           0       0       2       10      40
                    0.7           0       0       1       6       30
                    0.8           0       0       0       3       20
                    0.9           0       0       0       1       10
                    1.0 (right)   0       0       0       0       0
      
            ANALYSIS:
                  . Heat diffuses CONTINUOUSLY from left to right over time.
                  . At t=0: only left end is hot, rest is cold.
                  . Gradually heat spreads — temperature at each point
                    rises smoothly until steady state is reached.
                  . Steady state: perfect linear gradient 100C to 0C.
                  . PDE perfectly captures this spatial + temporal behavior.
                  . Discrete model CANNOT represent this — it has no
                    concept of spatial variation.






  ....................................
  C. NUMERICAL METHODS FOR SOLVING ODEs
  ....................................

    . Most real-world differential equations cannot be
      solved analytically (no neat formula).
    . Numerical methods APPROXIMATE the solution
      by computing small steps in time.

    . EULER'S METHOD (simplest numerical method):
      Y(t + h) = Y(t) + h . f(Y(t), t)
      where h = step size (small time increment)
      Start at known initial value, step forward repeatedly.

    . EXAMPLE — Euler's method on dT/dt = -0.05(T - 25):
      Initial: T(0) = 90C, step size h = 5 minutes




            Step  t(min)  T(t)     dT/dt = -0.05(T-25)    T_new = T + h.dT/dt
            ....................................................................
            0     0       90.0     -0.05 x (90-25) = -3.25  90 + 5(-3.25) = 73.75
            1     5       73.75    -0.05 x (73.75-25)=-2.44 73.75+5(-2.44)=61.56
            2     10      61.56    -0.05 x (61.56-25)=-1.83 61.56+5(-1.83)=52.41
            3     15      52.41    -0.05 x (52.41-25)=-1.37 52.41+5(-1.37)=45.56
            4     20      45.56    -0.05 x (45.56-25)=-1.03 45.56+5(-1.03)=40.43

                Compare with exact solution at t=20: T = 25 + 65.e^(-1) = 48.9C
                Euler gives 40.43C — some error due to large step size h=5.
                Smaller h = more accurate (but more computation).
          
              . RUNGE-KUTTA (RK4) is more accurate than Euler
                and is the standard method used in most simulators.

3.3  ANALOG COMPUTER
------------------------------------------------------------

  . WHAT IS AN ANALOG COMPUTER?
  .............................

    . A computing device that uses CONTINUOUS PHYSICAL
      quantities (voltage, current, mechanical position)
      to represent and solve mathematical problems.

    . Unlike digital computers which use 0s and 1s,
      analog computers work with smooth, continuous signals.

    . Voltage level directly represents the value of
      a variable in the differential equation.

    . If voltage = 5V, it might represent temperature = 50C
      or population = 5000 or velocity = 5 m/s.

    . Analog computers were the PRIMARY tool for continuous
      simulation before powerful digital computers existed
      (1940s through 1970s).

    . Used in: aircraft design, missile guidance,
      nuclear reactor simulation, chemical plant control.


  . HOW AN ANALOG COMPUTER WORKS:
  ................................

    . Uses electronic OPERATIONAL AMPLIFIERS (Op-Amps)
      as building blocks.

    . Key circuit components:

      INTEGRATOR:
        . Performs mathematical integration over time.
        . If input = dX/dt (rate of change),
          output = X (the actual value).
        . Core component — almost every ODE needs integration.

      SUMMER (Adder):
        . Adds multiple input voltages together.
        . Output = V1 + V2 + V3 + ...
        . Used to combine multiple terms in an equation.

      INVERTER:
        . Multiplies input voltage by -1.
        . Changes sign of a variable.

      MULTIPLIER:
        . Multiplies two voltages together.
        . Used for nonlinear terms like Y squared.

      COEFFICIENT POTENTIOMETER:
        . Scales a voltage by a constant factor.
        . Used to multiply a variable by a constant coefficient.

    . SCENARIO — Simulating Newton's Cooling on Analog Computer:
      Equation: dT/dt = -0.05 (T - 25)
      Rewrite:  dT/dt = -0.05T + 1.25

      Circuit setup:
      . Set initial voltage = 9V  (represents T=90C, scale: 1V=10C)
      . Integrator input connected to dT/dt signal
      . Integrator output gives T(t) as a continuous voltage
      . Summer combines -0.05T and +1.25 to give dT/dt
      . Oscilloscope displays the output voltage over time
        = displays the cooling curve directly on screen

      As the circuit runs, voltage at integrator output
      falls smoothly from 9V toward 2.5V (representing
      cooling from 90C toward 25C). ✓


  . ADVANTAGES OF ANALOG COMPUTERS:
  ..................................

    . SPEED: compute in REAL TIME — no discrete time steps.
    . NATURAL: physical processes are inherently analog.
    . PARALLEL: all variables computed simultaneously.
    . VISUAL: output directly visible on oscilloscope.


  . DISADVANTAGES OF ANALOG COMPUTERS:
  .....................................

    . LIMITED PRECISION: typically 3-4 significant figures.
    . SETUP TIME: must physically rewire circuits for each problem.
    . NOISE: electronic noise introduces errors.
    . NO MEMORY: cannot store results digitally.
    . SCALE PROBLEMS: must scale variables to fit voltage range.
    . OBSOLETE: mostly replaced by digital computers today.

3.4  ANALOG METHODS
------------------------------------------------------------

  . Analog methods refer to the TECHNIQUES used to set up
    and solve differential equations on analog computers.

  . Key procedures:

  ....................................
  A. PROBLEM SCALING
  ....................................

    . Variables in the real problem must be SCALED to fit
      within the voltage range of the analog computer
      (typically -10V to +10V).

    . If population P ranges from 0 to 10,000,000:
      Scale: 1V = 1,000,000 people
      So P = 3,000,000 is represented as 3V on the computer.

    . Time may also be scaled:
      1 second of computer time = 1 year of real time.
      This lets the computer simulate years of population
      growth in just seconds.

    . EXAMPLE:
      Real variable range     Scaled voltage range
      ....................................................
      Temperature: 0-100C     0V to 10V (1V = 10C)
      Population: 0-10M       0V to 10V (1V = 1M people)
      Velocity: 0-100 m/s     0V to 10V (1V = 10 m/s)
      Time: 0-60 min          0-6 sec   (1 sec = 10 min)


  ....................................
  B. AMPLITUDE SCALING
  ....................................

    . Ensures no variable exceeds the maximum voltage
      of the amplifiers (overloading = incorrect results).
    . Scale factors chosen so maximum expected value
      maps to about 80% of maximum voltage (safety margin).


  ....................................
  C. TIME SCALING
  ....................................

    . Fast real systems (missile flight in 0.5 seconds)
      slowed down on computer for observation.
    . Slow real systems (population growth over 100 years)
      sped up on computer to see result quickly.

    . SLOW TIME SCALE: 1 machine second = 0.001 real seconds
      (to study fast phenomena like explosions)
    . FAST TIME SCALE: 1 machine second = 1 real year
      (to study slow phenomena like climate change)


  ....................................
  D. EQUATION PREPARATION
  ....................................

    . Differential equation rearranged so the HIGHEST
      derivative is isolated on the left side.
    . Then integrated successively to get lower derivatives
      and finally the original variable.

    . EXAMPLE — Spring-mass system:
      Original: m.d²x/dt² + c.dx/dt + k.x = 0
      Rearrange: d²x/dt² = -(c/m).dx/dt - (k/m).x

      Analog circuit:
      1. Start with d²x/dt² (acceleration)
      2. Integrate once → dx/dt (velocity)
      3. Integrate again → x (position)
      4. Feed x and dx/dt back with coefficients
         to produce d²x/dt² (FEEDBACK LOOP)
      5. Connect oscilloscope to x output → see motion

3.5  HYBRID SIMULATION
------------------------------------------------------------

  . WHAT IS HYBRID SIMULATION?
  ............................

    . A simulation approach that COMBINES both analog
      and digital computing elements in one system.

    . Some parts of the problem are solved on analog hardware
      (for speed and continuity).
    . Other parts are handled by digital computer
      (for precision, logic, and control).

    . The two subsystems exchange data through
      ANALOG-TO-DIGITAL (A/D) and DIGITAL-TO-ANALOG (D/A) converters.


            . WHY HYBRID? THE BEST OF BOTH WORLDS:
            .......................................
          
                      .......................................................
                      ANALOG                    DIGITAL
                      .......................................................
                      Continuous, fast          Precise, programmable
                      No rounding errors        Logic and branching possible
                      Real-time parallel        Stores results digitally
                      computation               Can change parameters easily
                      Limited precision         Slower for differential eqs
                      Hard to reprogram         Easy to modify
                      .......................................................
          
              . Hybrid uses ANALOG for the continuous math-heavy parts
                and DIGITAL for control, logic, and precision parts.


      
        . SCENARIO — Aircraft Flight Simulation:
        .........................................
      
          . Aerodynamic equations (lift, drag, thrust, moment)
            involve continuous differential equations — handled
            by ANALOG section (fast, real-time).
      
          . Navigation computer, cockpit display, pilot input
            processing, mission logic — handled by DIGITAL section
            (programmed, precise, easy to update).
      
          . A/D converter: reads analog sensor values and sends
            to digital computer every millisecond.
      
          . D/A converter: digital computer sends control signals
            back to analog section to adjust parameters.
      
          . Together they simulate the complete aircraft system
            better than either could alone.
      




        . SCENARIO — Nuclear Reactor Simulation:
        .........................................
      
          . Neutron flux and heat equations = continuous, fast-changing
            → solved on ANALOG section.
      
          . Safety systems, alarm logic, operator controls,
            data logging = need digital precision and programming
            → handled on DIGITAL section.
      
          . Result: realistic real-time simulation of reactor
            behavior that no pure digital computer in the 1960s
            could achieve fast enough.
      




  . MODERN HYBRID SIMULATION:
  ...........................
  
      . Today, most "hybrid" simulation refers to combining
        DISCRETE EVENT simulation with CONTINUOUS simulation
        in the same software model.
  
      . Example: A manufacturing plant where:
        . Machine heating = continuous (differential equations)
        . Parts arriving on conveyor = discrete events
        . Software handles both in the same simulation run.
  
      . Tools: AnyLogic, MATLAB/Simulink support this.

3.6  DIGITAL-ANALOG SIMULATORS
------------------------------------------------------------

  . WHAT ARE DIGITAL-ANALOG SIMULATORS?
  .....................................

    . SOFTWARE programs running on digital computers that
      EMULATE the behavior of analog computers.

    . They solve differential equations numerically (using
      Euler, Runge-Kutta, etc.) but present results in a
      way similar to analog computer output.

    . Also called: Continuous System Simulation Languages (CSSL).


  . WHY DIGITAL-ANALOG SIMULATORS?
  .................................

    . Analog computers became impractical:
      . Expensive hardware required
      . Difficult to reprogram (must rewire circuits)
      . Limited precision
      . Hard to share or reproduce results

    . Digital-analog simulators gave the ADVANTAGES of
      analog thinking (define equations, connect blocks)
      on DIGITAL hardware (precise, programmable, storable).


  . FAMOUS DIGITAL-ANALOG SIMULATION LANGUAGES:
  ..............................................

    . CSMP (Continuous System Modeling Program) — IBM, 1960s
    . DYNAMO — system dynamics, used for World3 model
    . ACSL (Advanced Continuous Simulation Language)
    . MATLAB/Simulink — modern industry standard
    . STELLA — educational system dynamics tool
    . Modelica — modern open standard


  . HOW THEY WORK — BLOCK DIAGRAM APPROACH:
  ..........................................

    . User draws a BLOCK DIAGRAM connecting mathematical
      operations (just like wiring an analog computer).

    . Common blocks available:
      . INTEG  — integrator (INT block)
      . DERIV  — derivative
      . GAIN   — multiply by constant
      . SUM    — add multiple inputs
      . LIMIT  — limit output to a range
      . DEAD   — deadband (zero output in a range)
      . TABLE  — lookup table for nonlinear functions

    . EXAMPLE — CSMP code for Newton's Cooling:

      INCON  T0 = 90.0
      PARAM  K = 0.05, TENV = 25.0
      RATE   = -K * (TEMP - TENV)
      TEMP   = INTEG(RATE, T0)
      TIMER  FINTIM = 90.0, DELT = 0.5
      OUTPUT TEMP
      END

      . INTEG function integrates RATE starting from T0.
      . TEMP is computed at each DELT time step.
      . After 90 minutes, output is printed or plotted.

    . SIMULINK (MATLAB) equivalent:
      . Drag an Integrator block onto the canvas.
      . Connect a Gain block (coefficient -0.05).
      . Connect a Sum block with constant input (+1.25).
      . Run simulation, plot output automatically.


  . DATA EXAMPLE — Output from Digital Simulator:
  ................................................

    Simulation of cooling equation with DELT = 0.5 min:

    Time    Simulated T    Exact T     Error
    (min)   (C)            (C)         (C)
    ................................................
    0       90.000         90.000      0.000
    5       75.724         75.701      0.023
    10      64.408         64.407      0.001
    20      48.923         48.897      0.026
    30      39.485         39.460      0.025
    60      28.230         28.182      0.048
    90      25.720         25.717      0.003

    ANALYSIS:
    . Error is very small (< 0.05C) with DELT = 0.5 min.
    . Smaller DELT gives less error but takes more computation.
    . Digital simulator matches exact solution closely. ✓

3.7  EXAMPLE: A SERIAL CHASE PROBLEM
------------------------------------------------------------

  . WHAT IS A SERIAL CHASE PROBLEM?
  ..................................

    . A CLASSIC continuous simulation problem.
    . Object A is CHASING Object B which is moving.
    . A always moves DIRECTLY TOWARD B's current position.
    . B moves in a straight line at constant speed.
    . Question: Does A catch B? When? Where?

    . This is a PURSUIT CURVE problem — a well-known
      type of continuous system with coupled ODEs.

    . Used to model: missile tracking a target,
      predator chasing prey, homing torpedo,
      dog chasing a rabbit.


  . PROBLEM SETUP:
  ...............

    . Object B (TARGET):
      . Moves along the Y-axis in a straight line.
      . Starts at position (d, 0) — distance d from origin.
      . Speed = vB (constant, moving in +Y direction).

    . Object A (CHASER):
      . Starts at origin (0, 0).
      . Always points directly toward B's current position.
      . Speed = vA (constant, always toward B).


  . SPECIFIC NUMERICAL EXAMPLE:
  .............................

    . Setup:
      . A starts at (0, 0)
      . B starts at (100, 0) — 100 meters away on X-axis
      . B moves in +Y direction at vB = 10 m/s
      . A chases B at vA = 15 m/s (A is 50% faster)

    . At any instant, A moves directly toward B.
    . If A is at position (xA, yA) and B is at (xB, yB):
      . Direction vector from A to B = (xB-xA, yB-yA)
      . A moves along this direction at speed vA.


  . SIMULATION DATA TABLE:
  ........................

    Simulating with time step 1 second:

    Time  A position     B position     Distance A-B  A caught B?
    (s)   (xA, yA)       (xB, yB)       (m)
    ................................................................
    0     (0.0, 0.0)     (100.0, 0.0)   100.0         No
    5     (53.0, 12.5)   (100.0, 50.0)  62.3          No
    10    (83.4, 35.6)   (100.0, 100.0) 68.4          No
    15    (95.8, 72.1)   (100.0, 150.0) 78.1          No
    20    (99.2, 125.4)  (100.0, 200.0) 74.6          No
    25    (99.8, 187.5)  (100.0, 250.0) 62.5          No
    30    (100.0, 250.0) (100.0, 300.0) 50.0          No
    40    (100.0, 400.0) (100.0, 400.0) 0.0           YES! ✓

    ANALYSIS:
    . A initially shoots toward where B is, not where B will be.
    . A first moves mostly horizontally, then curves upward.
    . A approaches the same X coordinate as B quickly.
    . Then both travel vertically — A catching up from behind.
    . A catches B at approximately t = 40 seconds.
    . Catch position: approximately (100, 400).

    . If vA = vB (same speed): A NEVER catches B.
    . If vA < vB: A falls further and further behind.
    . If vA > vB: A always catches B eventually.


  . ANALYTICAL RESULT:
  ....................

    . When A starts at (d, 0) and B moves along Y-axis:
    . If k = vA/vB (speed ratio):

      Time to catch = (d/vB) . k / (k² - 1)   when k > 1

    . With d=100, vA=15, vB=10:
      k = 15/10 = 1.5
      T_catch = (100/10) . 1.5 / (1.5² - 1)
              = 10 . 1.5 / (2.25 - 1)
              = 15 / 1.25
              = 12 seconds  (for simplified setup)

    . NOTE: The exact time depends on the specific geometry.
      The simulation confirms the analytical prediction. ✓


  . WHY THIS IS A GOOD CONTINUOUS SIMULATION EXAMPLE:
  ....................................................

    . Position of A changes CONTINUOUSLY (not in jumps).
    . Direction of A changes CONTINUOUSLY as B moves.
    . The coupled ODEs cannot be solved easily by hand.
    . Simulation gives accurate answer step by step.
    . Demonstrates power of continuous simulation for
      PURSUIT and INTERCEPTION problems.

3.8  FEEDBACK SYSTEMS
------------------------------------------------------------

  . WHAT IS A FEEDBACK SYSTEM?
  ............................

    . A system where the OUTPUT is FED BACK as INPUT
      to influence or control future behavior.

    . The system MONITORS its own output and ADJUSTS
      its behavior based on what it measures.

    . Feedback is what makes systems SELF-REGULATING
      and SELF-CORRECTING.

    . Feedback is EVERYWHERE in real systems:
      . Thermostat controls room temperature
      . Cruise control maintains vehicle speed
      . Body temperature regulation (sweating, shivering)
      . Stock market prices responding to demand
      . Predator-prey population cycles


  . TWO TYPES OF FEEDBACK:
  ........................

    ....................................
    A. NEGATIVE FEEDBACK (Stabilizing)
    ....................................

      . Output is compared to a TARGET (setpoint).
      . If output > target: system reduces its action.
      . If output < target: system increases its action.
      . Result: output is DRIVEN TOWARD the target.
      . System is STABLE and SELF-CORRECTING.

      . EXAMPLE — THERMOSTAT:
        Target temperature = 22C.
        Room temperature rises to 25C (output > target).
        Feedback: send signal to turn OFF heating.
        Room cools toward 22C. ✓
        If room drops to 19C (output < target):
        Feedback: turn ON heating.
        System oscillates around 22C stably.

      . DIAGRAM:
        Setpoint (22C) → [+] → Controller → Heater
                          ↑                    ↓
                          └───── [Room Temp] ←─┘
                                 (feedback)

      . DIFFERENTIAL EQUATION for Thermostat:
        dT/dt = H(t) - k(T - T_env)
        where H(t) = heat input (ON or OFF based on feedback)

      . DATA EXAMPLE:
        Target = 22C, k = 0.1, T_env = 15C

        Time(min)  Room Temp(C)  Heater    Feedback Action
        .....................................................
        0          18.0          ON        Temp below 22C
        5          20.5          ON        Still below 22C
        10         22.3          OFF       Above 22C, turn off
        15         21.8          ON        Below 22C, turn on
        20         22.1          OFF       Above 22C, turn off
        25         21.9          ON        Small oscillation
        30         22.0          ---       Stabilized at 22C ✓

        System oscillates around setpoint then stabilizes.
        NEGATIVE FEEDBACK = STABILIZATION. ✓


    ....................................
    B. POSITIVE FEEDBACK (Destabilizing)
    ....................................

      . Output AMPLIFIES the input further.
      . More output → even more input → even more output.
      . Result: system grows EXPONENTIALLY or collapses.
      . System is UNSTABLE — goes to extreme values.

      . EXAMPLE — POPULATION EXPLOSION:
        More people → more births → even more people.
        dP/dt = r . P   (r positive)
        Output (P) feeds back to increase rate of growth.
        Positive feedback → exponential growth.

      . EXAMPLE — BANK PANIC:
        Some customers withdraw money →
        Others hear about it and withdraw too →
        More fear → more withdrawals → bank collapses.
        Each withdrawal increases the next.

      . EXAMPLE — MICROPHONE FEEDBACK (Howling):
        Sound from speaker → enters microphone →
        amplified → louder from speaker →
        enters microphone again at higher volume →
        grows louder and louder → screech. ✓

      . DATA EXAMPLE — Positive Feedback in Epidemic:
        Infected people (I) infect Susceptible people (S).
        More infected → more new infections → even more infected.
        dI/dt = beta . I . S

        Day   I (Infected)   S (Susceptible)   New cases
        .....................................................
        0     10             990               ~10
        5     50             950               ~48
        10    200            800               ~160
        15    600            400               ~240
        20    900            100               ~90
        25    990            10                ~10
        30    999            1                 ~1

        Initially: explosive positive feedback growth.
        Eventually: slows as susceptible people run out.
        Classic S-shaped epidemic curve. ✓


  . FEEDBACK IN CONTINUOUS SIMULATION:
  .....................................

    . Feedback creates LOOPS in the differential equation system.
    . The output variable appears on the right-hand side
      of the equation (it feeds back into the rate equation).

    . GENERAL FEEDBACK ODE:
      dX/dt = f(X, inputs)
      X appears in f → FEEDBACK LOOP.

    . PREDATOR-PREY EXAMPLE (Lotka-Volterra equations):
      Rabbits (R): dR/dt = a.R - b.R.F
      Foxes (F):   dF/dt = c.R.F - d.F

      . More rabbits → more food for foxes → foxes grow.
      . More foxes → more rabbits eaten → rabbits decline.
      . Fewer rabbits → less food → foxes decline.
      . Fewer foxes → rabbits recover.
      . Endless cycle of mutual feedback. ✓

      DATA SIMULATION (a=1.0, b=0.1, c=0.075, d=1.5):

      Time  Rabbits  Foxes   What is happening
      ..................................................
      0     40       9       Initial populations
      2     50       12      Rabbits growing, foxes growing
      4     30       18      Rabbits declining (too many foxes)
      6     10       10      Both declining
      8     15       5       Foxes low, rabbits recovering
      10    35       4       Rabbits recovered, foxes still low
      12    50       8       Cycle repeating
      14    42       12      Back near start — oscillation ✓

      ANALYSIS:
      . System oscillates continuously — never reaches steady state.
      . This is MUTUAL NEGATIVE FEEDBACK creating oscillation.
      . Neither species goes extinct (in this idealized model).
      . Continuous simulation perfectly captures this cycle.
      . Discrete model cannot represent this smooth oscillation.




------------------------------------------------------------
  ANALYSIS: WHEN TO USE CONTINUOUS SIMULATION
------------------------------------------------------------

  . USE CONTINUOUS SIMULATION WHEN:
  .................................

    . System state changes SMOOTHLY over time.
    . System is described by RATES OF CHANGE (derivatives).
    . Physical or natural processes are involved.
    . FEEDBACK LOOPS are present in the system.
    . Spatial variation matters (PDEs needed).

  . DO NOT USE for:
  .................

    . Events that happen at specific moments (use discrete).
    . Queue-based systems (use discrete event simulation).
    . Systems with sudden jumps or discontinuities (use hybrid).

------------------------------------------------------------
  COMPLETE SUMMARY TABLE — UNIT III
------------------------------------------------------------

  .......................................................
  Topic               Key Point
  .......................................................
  Continuous System   State changes smoothly over time.
                      Described by differential equations.
                      Not by discrete events.

  ODE                 Ordinary Differential Equation.
                      One independent variable (time).
                      dY/dt = f(Y,t).
                      Example: population growth, cooling.

  PDE                 Partial Differential Equation.
                      Multiple variables (time AND space).
                      dT/dt = alpha.d2T/dx2 (heat equation).
                      Distributed parameter systems.

  Euler Method        Numerical ODE solver.
                      Y_new = Y + h . f(Y,t)
                      Simple but less accurate.
                      Smaller h = more accurate.

  Analog Computer     Uses voltage to represent variables.
                      Op-amps: integrators, summers, inverters.
                      Solves ODEs in real time.
                      Fast but imprecise and hard to reprogram.

  Problem Scaling     Mapping real variable ranges to
                      voltage range (-10V to +10V).
                      Amplitude scaling + time scaling.

  Hybrid Simulation   Combines analog (continuous, fast) with
                      digital (precise, logical).
                      A/D and D/A converters link them.
                      Used in aircraft, nuclear, defense.

  Digital-Analog      Software on digital computer that
  Simulator           emulates analog computer.
                      CSMP, DYNAMO, MATLAB/Simulink.
                      Block diagram approach.

  Serial Chase        A chases B which moves in a line.
                      A always points toward B's position.
                      Coupled ODEs describe the pursuit.
                      A catches B only if vA > vB.

  Negative Feedback   Output compared to target.
                      Corrects deviation. Stabilizing.
                      Example: thermostat, cruise control.

  Positive Feedback   Output amplifies itself.
                      Grows or collapses. Destabilizing.
                      Example: population growth, bank panic.

  Lotka-Volterra      Predator-prey coupled ODEs.
                      Mutual feedback creates oscillation.
                      Neither species extinct in ideal model.
  .......................................................

  KEY FORMULAS REFERENCE

  NEWTON'S COOLING:
  . dT/dt = -k(T - T_env)
  . T(t) = T_env + (T0 - T_env).e^(-k.t)

  POPULATION GROWTH:
  . dP/dt = r.P
  . P(t) = P0.e^(r.t)

  EULER METHOD:
  . Y(t+h) = Y(t) + h . f(Y(t), t)

  LOTKA-VOLTERRA:
  . dR/dt = a.R - b.R.F
  . dF/dt = c.R.F - d.F

  SERIAL CHASE CATCH TIME:
  . T_catch = (d/vB) . k / (k^2 - 1)
  . where k = vA/vB > 1

  HEAT EQUATION (PDE):
  . dT/dt = alpha . d2T/dx2
