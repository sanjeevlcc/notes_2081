------------------------------------------------------------
  WHY CLOUD SECURITY MATTERS
------------------------------------------------------------

  . Cloud computing moves an organization's data,
    applications, and infrastructure OUTSIDE its own walls.

    Data no longer sits in a locked server room —
    it lives in a shared data center managed by Amazon,
    Google, or Microsoft.




  . GLOBAL SECURITY STATISTICS (2024-2025):
   ---------------------------------------------
    . Average cost of a data breach globally: USD 4.88 million.
    . 45% of all data breaches now involve cloud assets.
    . 80% of organizations suffered at least one cloud
      security incident in the past 18 months.
    . Misconfiguration is the #1 cause of cloud breaches.



  . NEPAL CONTEXT:
   ---------------------------------------------
    . Nepal Rastra Bank (NRB) issued cloud security directives
      for banks and financial institutions.
    . eSewa, Khalti, and IME Pay process millions of transactions
      — a single breach could expose all customer financial data.
    . Government of Nepal's Digital Nepal Framework requires
      public cloud data to meet local storage requirements.





  . THE SHARED RESPONSIBILITY MODEL (Key concept):
   ---------------------------------------------
    . In cloud computing, BOTH the cloud provider AND
      the customer share security responsibilities.
    . Provider secures: physical hardware, data centers,
      network infrastructure, hypervisors.
    . Customer secures: data, applications, user access,
      configuration, identity management.
    . Most breaches happen because CUSTOMERS fail their
      part — not because AWS or Azure were hacked.




                .......................................................
                Responsibility   IaaS          PaaS          SaaS
                .......................................................
                Physical DC      Provider      Provider      Provider
                Network infra    Provider      Provider      Provider
                Hypervisor       Provider      Provider      Provider
                OS               Customer      Provider      Provider
                Runtime/App      Customer      Provider      Provider
                Data             Customer      Customer      Customer
                Identity (IAM)   Customer      Customer      Customer
                Configuration    Customer      Customer      Customer
                .......................................................




    LESSON: Even in SaaS (Office 365, Gmail),
    the CUSTOMER is responsible for WHO can access the data.
    If an employee shares a sensitive folder publicly —
    that is the customer's failure, not Microsoft's.

7.1  CLOUD SECURITY ISSUES AND CHALLENGES
------------------------------------------------------------

  ....................................
  A. TOP CLOUD SECURITY THREATS
  ....................................

    1. DATA BREACHES
       . Unauthorized access to sensitive/confidential data.
       . CAUSE: weak passwords, stolen credentials, misconfiguration.

       . EXAMPLE — Capital One Breach (2019):
         A former AWS employee exploited a misconfigured
         Web Application Firewall (WAF) to access 100 million
         customer records stored in Amazon S3.

         Cost: USD 80 million fine + reputation damage.
         ROOT CAUSE: misconfiguration (customer's responsibility).

       . DATA EXAMPLE — Categories of exposed data:
               .....................................................
               Data Type          Records Exposed   Sensitivity
               .....................................................
               Credit card info   140,000           Critical
               SSN / National ID  80,000            Critical
               Bank account       1 million         Very High
               Email addresses    100 million       High
               Home addresses     100 million       High
               .....................................................

    2. MISCONFIGURATION
       . Most common cause of cloud security incidents.
       . Accidental exposure of storage buckets (S3, Azure Blob)
         to the public internet.

       . Example: company accidentally sets S3 bucket as
         "public read" → entire database exposed to anyone.

       . REAL INCIDENTS FROM OPEN BUCKETS:
         . Twitch: 125 GB of source code leaked.
         . Accenture: 137 GB of client data exposed.
         . US Department of Defense: 1.8 billion surveillance
           posts exposed on unprotected Elasticsearch server.



    3. INSECURE APIs
       . Cloud services are accessed via APIs (Application
         Programming Interfaces).

       . Weak or unprotected APIs = direct path to data.
       . Missing authentication, no rate limiting,
         no input validation.



    4. ACCOUNT HIJACKING
       . Attackers steal cloud account credentials.
       . Phishing emails targeting cloud admin accounts.

       . Once inside: steal data, run crypto mining,
         delete backups, launch attacks from your account.

       . Scenario: Nepal bank employee receives email
         "Your AWS account has been suspended — click here."
         Employee enters credentials → attacker logs in
         → shuts down all servers or steals all data.



    5. DENIAL OF SERVICE (DoS / DDoS)
       . Flood a cloud service with traffic until it crashes.
       . Distributed DoS: millions of infected devices attack
         one target simultaneously.

       . Cloud providers offer DDoS protection (AWS Shield,
         Azure DDoS Protection, Cloudflare).



    6. INSIDER THREATS
       . Malicious or careless employees with legitimate access.
       . They already have the credentials — no hacking needed.

       . Example: database administrator exports entire
         customer table and sells it to competitor.




    7. SUPPLY CHAIN ATTACKS
       . Attacking a SUPPLIER of a target organization.
       . SolarWinds attack (2020): hackers inserted malware
         into software update — reached 18,000 organizations
         including US government agencies via a trusted supplier.




  ....................................
  B. CLOUD SECURITY CHALLENGES
  ....................................

    1. VISIBILITY PROBLEM:
       . In traditional IT: security team can see ALL traffic
         flowing through their own network switches.

       . In cloud: traffic happens INSIDE the cloud provider's
         infrastructure — customer has limited visibility.
       . Solution: Cloud-native monitoring tools (CloudTrail,
         Azure Monitor).




    2. DYNAMIC AND EPHEMERAL RESOURCES:
       . Cloud resources spin up and down in seconds.
       . A server that existed for 5 minutes may have been
         compromised and deleted — no logs remain.

       . Traditional security tools designed for static infra
         don't work in this dynamic environment.




    3. MULTI-CLOUD COMPLEXITY:
       . Organizations use AWS for some workloads, Azure for others,
         GCP for analytics.

       . Each cloud has its OWN security model, tools, terminology.
       . Security team must master all three simultaneously.




    4. COMPLIANCE IN MULTIPLE JURISDICTIONS:
       . Data stored in AWS us-east-1 is physically in USA.

       . European customer data stored in USA may violate
         EU GDPR (General Data Protection Regulation).
       . Where is the data? Which country's laws apply?



    5. SHADOW IT:
       . Employees or teams use cloud services WITHOUT IT approval.

       . Marketing team signs up for a cloud tool, uploads
         customer data — IT never knew.
       . Data in unapproved, unmonitored, unsecured services.

7.2  IDENTITY AND ACCESS MANAGEMENT (IAM)
------------------------------------------------------------

  . WHAT IS IAM?
  .............

    . IAM = the system that controls WHO can access WHAT,
      WHEN, and HOW within a cloud environment.

    . Answers three security questions:
            . Authentication: WHO are you? (prove your identity)
            . Authorization:  WHAT can you do? (your permissions)
            . Accounting:     WHAT did you do? (audit logs)

    . IAM is the FOUNDATION of cloud security.
      If identity is compromised, everything else fails.



      
        ....................................
        A. AUTHENTICATION — PROVING YOUR IDENTITY
        ....................................
      
          . THREE FACTORS OF AUTHENTICATION:
      
            Factor 1 — SOMETHING YOU KNOW:
              . Password, PIN, security question.
              . Weakest factor — can be guessed, stolen, phished.
      
            Factor 2 — SOMETHING YOU HAVE:
              . Mobile phone (OTP), hardware token (YubiKey),
                smart card.
              . Stronger — attacker needs physical device too.
      
            Factor 3 — SOMETHING YOU ARE:
              . Fingerprint, face recognition, iris scan.
              . Biometrics — hardest to steal.




          . MFA — MULTI-FACTOR AUTHENTICATION:
                  . Requires 2 or more factors to log in.
                  . Even if password is stolen, attacker needs
                    the phone too to get the OTP.
                  . Microsoft reports MFA blocks 99.9% of
                    automated account attacks.




          . DATA EXAMPLE — Login attack scenarios:
                  .....................................................
                  Auth Method       Phishing   Brute Force   Credential
                                    Attack     Attack        Stuffing
                  .....................................................
                  Password only     Fails      Fails         Fails
                  Password + OTP    Blocks     Blocks        Blocks
                  Password + HW key Blocks     Blocks        Blocks
                  Biometric + PIN   Blocks     Blocks        Blocks
                  .....................................................
            MFA blocks ALL common attack types. ✓
      
      
        ....................................
        B. AUTHORIZATION — WHAT CAN YOU DO?
        ....................................
      
          . RBAC — ROLE-BASED ACCESS CONTROL:
            . Assign permissions to ROLES, not individuals.
            . Users are assigned roles.
            . Simpler management: change role → user's permissions change.




            EXAMPLE — Hospital cloud system:
                  .....................................................
                  Role           Permissions
                  .....................................................
                  Doctor         Read patient records
                                 Write diagnosis notes
                                 Prescribe medications
                  Nurse          Read patient records
                                 Update vital signs only
                  Receptionist   Read appointment schedules
                                 Create/edit appointments
                                 No access to medical records
                  IT Admin       Manage user accounts
                                 No access to patient records
                  Auditor        Read-only access to all logs
                                 No data modification
                  .....................................................



          . PRINCIPLE OF LEAST PRIVILEGE:
                  . Give users ONLY the minimum permissions needed for their job.
                  . Developer should NOT have delete permissions on production database.
                  . Reduces damage if account is compromised.
            
            BAD PRACTICE:
                    Developer accidentally deletes production database.
                    Why? Developer had admin rights "just in case."
                    Entire system down for 6 hours.
      
            GOOD PRACTICE:
                    Developer has read access to dev database only.
                    Separate admin account (with MFA) for production.
                    Accidental deletion impossible from developer account.
            
          . ABAC — ATTRIBUTE-BASED ACCESS CONTROL:
                  . More granular than RBAC.
                  . Access based on: user attributes + resource attributes +
                    environment conditions.
                  . Example: "Doctor can access patient records IF
                    the doctor is assigned to that patient AND
                    it is during business hours AND
                    access is from a hospital-approved device."
      
      
        ....................................
        C. AWS IAM — PRACTICAL EXAMPLE
        ....................................
      
          . In AWS, IAM has four key entities:
                  . Users:    individual human accounts.
                  . Groups:   collection of users with same permissions.
                  . Roles:    temporary identities for services or apps.
                  . Policies: JSON documents defining permissions.



          . EXAMPLE IAM POLICY (JSON):
                  Policy: "Allow reading S3 bucket, deny deletion"
                  ....................................................
                        {
                          "Version": "2012-10-17",
                          "Statement": [
                            {
                              "Effect": "Allow",
                              "Action": ["s3:GetObject", "s3:ListBucket"],
                              "Resource": "arn:aws:s3:::company-data/*"
                            },
                            {
                              "Effect": "Deny",
                              "Action": "s3:DeleteObject",
                              "Resource": "arn:aws:s3:::company-data/*"
                            }
                          ]
                        }
                  ....................................................


            This policy: developers can read and list S3 objects.
            Deletion is explicitly DENIED — even if another policy
            grants it, Deny always wins in AWS IAM.



          . IAM ROLES FOR SERVICES:
                  . An EC2 instance (virtual server) needs to read from S3.
                  . Instead of hardcoding AWS credentials in the code
                    (BAD PRACTICE — anyone who reads the code gets credentials!),
                    ASSIGN a ROLE to the EC2 instance.
                  . EC2 automatically gets temporary credentials.
                  . When role is removed, credentials expire. ✓

7.3  DATA SECURITY AND ENCRYPTION
------------------------------------------------------------

  . DATA STATES AND SECURITY:
          . Data at Rest:    stored on disk, database, S3 bucket.
          . Data in Transit: moving over network.
          . Data in Use:     being processed in memory.

    Each state requires different security approaches.

            
              ....................................
              A. ENCRYPTION — THE CORE OF DATA SECURITY
              ....................................
            
                . Encryption converts readable data (plaintext)
                  into unreadable scrambled form (ciphertext).
                . Only someone with the correct KEY can decrypt it.
                . Even if attacker steals the data, they cannot read it
                  without the key.
            
                . ANALOGY:
                        Data = a letter.
                        Encryption = locking it in a safe.
                        Attacker steals the safe — useless without the combination.
                        Key = the combination.
            
                . SYMMETRIC ENCRYPTION:
                        . SAME key used for encryption AND decryption.
                        . Fast — suitable for large data volumes.
                        . Algorithm: AES (Advanced Encryption Standard).
                          AES-256 = 256-bit key = 2^256 possible keys.
                          Brute force: would take longer than the age of the universe.
                        . Problem: how do you securely share the key with
                          the recipient? (Key distribution problem)
                        . Used for: encrypting files, disks, databases.
            
                . ASYMMETRIC ENCRYPTION:
                        . TWO keys: Public Key (share with everyone) and
                          Private Key (keep secret forever).
                        . Encrypted with PUBLIC KEY, decrypted with PRIVATE KEY.
                        . Solve key distribution: publish public key openly.
                        . Algorithm: RSA (Rivest-Shamir-Adleman).
                        . Slower than symmetric — not for large data.
                        . Used for: TLS (HTTPS), SSH, digital signatures,
                          exchanging the symmetric key securely.
            
                . HYBRID ENCRYPTION (How HTTPS works):
                        Step 1: Client gets server's PUBLIC KEY.
                        Step 2: Client generates a random SYMMETRIC KEY.
                        Step 3: Client encrypts that symmetric key with
                                server's PUBLIC KEY and sends it.
                        Step 4: Server decrypts using PRIVATE KEY.
                                Now both have the same symmetric key.
                        Step 5: All further communication encrypted with
                                fast SYMMETRIC AES encryption.
                  Result: security of asymmetric + speed of symmetric. ✓
            



              ....................................
              B. ENCRYPTION AT REST
              ....................................
            
                . Data stored on disk is encrypted.
                . If hard drive is physically stolen, data is unreadable.
            
                . AWS S3 Encryption options:
                        . SSE-S3: AWS manages keys (simplest, less control).
                        . SSE-KMS: AWS Key Management Service manages keys
                                   (audit trail of who used which key, when).
                        . SSE-C: Customer provides their own keys.
                                 AWS uses them but never stores them.
            
                . Full Disk Encryption:
                        . BitLocker (Windows), FileVault (Mac), LUKS (Linux).
                        . Entire disk encrypted — even OS.
                        . Used for: laptops, portable hard drives.
                  
                . DATABASE ENCRYPTION:
                        . Transparent Data Encryption (TDE) in SQL Server, Oracle.
                        . Data encrypted on disk, automatically decrypted on read.
                        . Application code changes not required.
                  
            
              ....................................
              C. ENCRYPTION IN TRANSIT
              ....................................
            
                . All data moving over the network must be encrypted.
                . TLS (Transport Layer Security) — HTTPS uses TLS.
                  All web traffic between browser and server encrypted.
            
                . SCENARIO — Unencrypted vs Encrypted:
                        WITHOUT HTTPS (HTTP only):
                                User types password "Nepal@123" on login page.
                                Data travels over network as: "Nepal@123" (plaintext).
                                Attacker on same Wi-Fi can read it with Wireshark.
                        
                        WITH HTTPS (TLS encrypted):
                                Same password travels as encrypted ciphertext.
                                Attacker intercepts: "xK9#mP2&qL1!zR4..."
                                Completely unreadable. ✓



                . VPN (Virtual Private Network):
                        . Creates encrypted tunnel over public internet.
                        . All traffic inside the tunnel is encrypted.
                        . Even if network is compromised, data is safe.
                        . Used for: remote workers connecting to company cloud.
            
            
              ....................................
              D. KEY MANAGEMENT
              ....................................
            
                . Encryption is only as good as KEY PROTECTION.
                . If attacker gets the key, encryption is useless.
            
                . KEY MANAGEMENT SERVICES (KMS):
                        . AWS KMS, Azure Key Vault, Google Cloud KMS.
                        . Centralized storage and management of encryption keys.
                        . Hardware Security Module (HSM): tamper-proof hardware
                          device that stores keys — keys never leave the HSM.
                        . Key rotation: change encryption keys regularly.
                          Even if old key is stolen, new data is safe.
                  
                . DATA EXAMPLE — Cost of poor key management:
                        Company encrypts 10 TB of customer data.
                        Stores the decryption key in a README.txt file
                        in the same S3 bucket as the data.
                        Attacker finds the bucket → reads README.txt →
                        has the key → decrypts all 10 TB.
                        Encryption provided ZERO protection.
                        Lesson: NEVER store the key with the data. ✓

7.4  APPLICATION AND VM SECURITY
------------------------------------------------------------

  ....................................
  A. APPLICATION SECURITY IN CLOUD
  ....................................

    . OWASP TOP 10 — Most Critical Web Application Vulnerabilities:
      (Applicable to cloud-hosted applications)

      1. BROKEN ACCESS CONTROL:
               Users can access data or functions they should not.
               Example: URL change exposes other user's account.
               Prevention: server-side authorization checks.

      2. INJECTION (SQL, Command):
               Attacker inserts malicious code into input fields.
               Classic example: login form with SQL injection.
      
               Normal query:
                     SELECT * FROM users WHERE username='admin' AND password='1234'
      
               Attacker inputs: admin' OR '1'='1
                     Resulting query:
                           SELECT * FROM users WHERE username='admin' OR '1'='1'
                           '1'='1' is ALWAYS TRUE → logged in without password!
            
                     Prevention: parameterized queries, input validation.

      3. CRYPTOGRAPHIC FAILURES:
               Sensitive data transmitted without encryption.
               Weak algorithms (MD5, SHA-1) used for passwords.
               Prevention: Always use TLS, use bcrypt for passwords.

      4. SECURITY MISCONFIGURATION:
               Default passwords, open ports, verbose error messages,
               publicly accessible S3 buckets.
               Prevention: security hardening checklist, CIS Benchmarks.

    . WEB APPLICATION FIREWALL (WAF):
            . Filters malicious HTTP requests before they reach the app.
            . AWS WAF, Azure Application Gateway WAF.
            . Blocks: SQL injection, Cross-Site Scripting (XSS),
              known attack patterns, specific IP ranges.


    . SECURE SDLC (Secure Software Development Lifecycle):
            . Integrate security at EVERY stage of development.
            . Not just testing at the end.
            . Security code review, SAST (Static Analysis),
              DAST (Dynamic Analysis), penetration testing.


  ....................................
  B. VIRTUAL MACHINE SECURITY
  ....................................

    . VMs in cloud face unique security challenges:

      1. VM IMAGE SECURITY:
               . Templates used to launch VMs may contain vulnerabilities.
               . Always use official, verified images from AWS AMI
                 Marketplace, Azure Marketplace, or your own hardened images.
               . Never use random third-party images — they may contain
                 pre-installed malware.

      2. PATCH MANAGEMENT:
               . VMs run operating systems (Linux, Windows).
               . OS vulnerabilities must be patched regularly.
               . Cloud does NOT patch your OS automatically in IaaS.
               . AWS Systems Manager Patch Manager automates patching.

      3. SECURITY GROUPS AND NETWORK ACLs:
               . Security Groups: virtual firewall for EC2 instances.
               . Control which ports are OPEN to which IPs.

               BAD CONFIGURATION (dangerous):
                     Port 22 (SSH) open to 0.0.0.0/0 (entire internet).
                     Anyone on earth can attempt to log into your server.
                     Brute force attacks will start within minutes.
      
               GOOD CONFIGURATION:
                     Port 22 open to 203.0.113.5/32 (only your office IP).
                     Port 443 open to 0.0.0.0/0 (HTTPS from everyone — needed).
                     All other ports: DENY by default.

      4. INSTANCE METADATA SERVICE:
               . VMs can query their own metadata (IP, role, credentials).
               . Attackers exploit Server-Side Request Forgery (SSRF)
                 to query metadata and steal credentials.
               . Mitigation: IMDSv2 (token-required metadata access).

      5. HYPERVISOR SECURITY:
               . VM ESCAPE: hypothetical attack where attacker breaks
                 out of a VM and accesses the hypervisor or other VMs.
               . Cloud providers invest heavily in preventing this.
               . AWS uses Nitro hypervisor — custom-built, minimal
                 attack surface, hardware-enforced isolation.

7.5  MULTI-TENANCY ISSUES
------------------------------------------------------------

  . WHAT IS MULTI-TENANCY?
  .......................

    . Multiple customers (tenants) share the SAME physical
      infrastructure in cloud computing.
    . Your EC2 instance might run on the same physical server
      as another company's EC2 instance.
    . Isolation between tenants is critical for security.

  ....................................
  A. MULTI-TENANCY RISKS
  ....................................

    1. DATA LEAKAGE BETWEEN TENANTS:
             . If isolation is imperfect, one tenant's data could
               be visible to another tenant.
             . Usually prevented by hypervisor-level separation.
             . Has happened in edge cases: Rowhammer attack (2015)
               allowed memory bit flipping across VM boundaries.

    2. NOISY NEIGHBOR PROBLEM:
             . Security perspective: one tenant consuming unusual
               resources may indicate cryptomining malware or
               DDoS botnet activity that also affects neighboring VMs.
             . Performance AND security concern.

    3. SHARED VULNERABILITY:
             . If a vulnerability exists in the hypervisor or cloud
               management plane, ALL tenants on that infrastructure
               are potentially affected simultaneously.
             . Spectre/Meltdown (2018): CPU vulnerabilities that
               allowed reading other processes' memory.
               Cloud providers had to patch ALL servers for ALL tenants.

    4. FORENSIC CHALLENGES:
             . If a security incident occurs, data from multiple
               tenants may be on the same physical disk.
             . Forensic investigation must not expose other tenants' data.
             . Cloud providers have strict access controls for forensics.

  ....................................
  B. ISOLATION MECHANISMS
  ....................................

    . HYPERVISOR ISOLATION:
      Each VM runs in its own isolated virtual environment.
      VMs cannot see each other's memory or disk.

    . NETWORK ISOLATION:
            . VPC (Virtual Private Cloud): isolated network space.
            . Your VMs communicate within your VPC.
            . Other tenants' VMs are in DIFFERENT VPCs.
            . VPCs cannot communicate unless explicitly connected.

    . DEDICATED INSTANCES:
            . VMs that run on hardware NOT shared with other AWS accounts.
            . More expensive — for compliance-sensitive workloads.

    . DEDICATED HOSTS:
            . Entire physical server dedicated to one customer.
            . No hardware sharing at all.
            . Used for: financial institutions, healthcare, defense.






  ....................................
  C. MULTI-TENANCY MODELS
  ....................................

          .......................................................
          Model           Isolation Level   Cost      Use Case
          .......................................................
          Shared tenant   Software only     Cheapest  General apps
          Dedicated inst  Hardware-isolated Higher    Compliance
          Dedicated host  Full server       Highest   Regulated data
          Private cloud   Separate infra    Most      Top security
          .......................................................





  ....................................
  D. TENANT DATA SEPARATION IN SaaS
  ....................................

    . SaaS applications must separate data between customers.

          APPROACH 1 — SEPARATE DATABASE PER TENANT:
            . Each customer gets their own database.
            . Strongest isolation, expensive at scale.
            . Example: each hospital gets its own database schema.
      
          APPROACH 2 — SHARED DATABASE, TENANT ID COLUMN:
            . All customers in one database table.
            . Every row has a tenant_id column.
            . Application filters by tenant_id on every query.
            . Risk: developer forgets the filter → data leakage!
            . Example bug: Hospital A sees Hospital B's patient records
              because a developer forgot WHERE tenant_id = 'HospA'.
      
          APPROACH 3 — SEPARATE SCHEMA PER TENANT:
            . One database, multiple schemas (hospital_a.patients,
              hospital_b.patients).
            . Middle ground between isolation and cost.

7.6  SECURITY MONITORING AND AUDITING
------------------------------------------------------------

  . WHAT IS SECURITY MONITORING?
  .............................

    . Continuously watching cloud systems for suspicious
      activity, anomalies, and security events.
    . Goal: DETECT attacks before or as they happen —
      not discover them weeks later.

    . STATISTICS:
            Average time to identify a data breach: 194 DAYS.
            Average time to contain a breach: 64 MORE DAYS.
            Total: 258 days from breach to containment.
            58% of breaches would not have been detected at all
            without monitoring tools.


  ....................................
  A. WHAT TO MONITOR
  ....................................

    . AUTHENTICATION EVENTS:
      . Failed logins (who is trying to get in?)
      . Logins from unusual locations (user in Nepal suddenly
        logs in from Russia 5 minutes later — impossible!)
      . Multiple failed logins then success = account compromise.

    . CONFIGURATION CHANGES:
      . New IAM user created (who authorized this?)
      . Security group rule changed (port opened)
      . S3 bucket made public (data exposure risk!)
      . New API key generated

    . DATA ACCESS:
      . Large data downloads (employee exfiltrating data?)
      . Access to sensitive data outside business hours.
      . Accessing data in unusual patterns.

    . NETWORK TRAFFIC:
      . Unusual outbound traffic (data exfiltration?).
      . Traffic to known malicious IP addresses.
      . Port scanning of other instances.

    . DATA EXAMPLE — Suspicious activity detection:
      Normal behavior for user "admin@company.com":
        Login time: 9 AM - 6 PM Nepal time
        Location:   Kathmandu IP range
        Actions:    Read files, create reports
        Data volume: < 100 MB per day

      Alert triggered when:
            ....................................................
            Anomaly                  Alert Level   Action
            ....................................................
            Login at 2 AM local      Medium        Notify security
            Login from Russia IP     High          Block + Notify
            200 GB download in 1 hr  Critical      Block immediately
            New admin user created   High          Require approval
            S3 bucket made public    Critical      Revert + Alert
            ....................................................




  ....................................
  B. CLOUD MONITORING TOOLS
  ....................................

    . AWS CLOUDTRAIL:
      . Records EVERY API call made in the AWS account.
      . Who did what, when, from which IP address.
      . "CCTV camera for your cloud account."
      . Stores logs in S3 for long-term retention.

            Example CloudTrail log entry:
            ....................................................
                        {
                          "eventTime": "2025-03-15T02:34:12Z",
                          "userIdentity": {"userName": "dev-user"},
                          "sourceIPAddress": "185.220.101.45",
                          "eventName": "DeleteBucket",
                          "requestParameters": {"bucketName": "prod-data"}
                        }
            ....................................................
    


      Interpretation: "dev-user" deleted a production bucket
            at 2 AM from a suspicious IP (Tor exit node).
            Action: immediate investigation, credential rotation.

    . AWS GUARDDUTY:
            . AI-powered threat detection service.
            . Analyzes CloudTrail, VPC Flow Logs, DNS logs.
            . Automatically detects: cryptocurrency mining,
              compromised instances, unusual API calls.
            . Generates "findings" with severity level.



    . AWS SECURITY HUB:
            . Aggregates security alerts from all AWS services.
            . Single dashboard for all security findings.
            . Checks against security standards (CIS, PCI DSS, GDPR).



    . SIEM — Security Information and Event Management:
            . Centralized log collection from all sources.
            . Correlates events across multiple systems.
            . Tools: Splunk, IBM QRadar, Microsoft Sentinel.
            . Example: login failure on 5 different servers in
              10 minutes from same IP = coordinated attack.
              SIEM correlates all 5 events and raises ONE alert.


  ....................................
  C. SECURITY AUDITING
  ....................................

          . AUDIT = formal review of security controls and activities.
          . Who accessed what data, when, and why?
          . Prove to regulators that security controls are in place.
          . Find gaps before attackers do.
      
          . TYPES OF AUDITS:
                  . Internal Audit: your own team reviews security posture.
                  . External Audit: independent third party reviews.
                    More credible — no conflict of interest.
                  . Penetration Testing (Pen Test): ethical hackers try
                    to break into your system. Find vulnerabilities first.
                  . Vulnerability Assessment: automated scanning for
                    known security weaknesses.

          . AUDIT TRAIL REQUIREMENTS:
                  . All privileged access logged.
                  . Logs must be TAMPER-PROOF (stored separately, immutable).
                  . Retained for defined period (1 year minimum typically).
                  . Include: who, what, when, where, outcome.

7.7  LEGAL AND REGULATORY ASPECTS
------------------------------------------------------------

  . Cloud computing involves data crossing geographic boundaries.
  . Data subject to laws of WHERE IT IS STORED, not just where
    the company is headquartered.
  . Understanding the legal landscape is REQUIRED for compliance.


  ....................................
  A. KEY REGULATIONS
  ....................................

    1. GDPR — GENERAL DATA PROTECTION REGULATION (EU):
             . European Union law, effective May 2018.
             . Applies to ANY organization processing EU citizens' data,
               regardless of where the company is located.
               (Nepali company serving European customers → GDPR applies!)

       KEY REQUIREMENTS:
             . Lawful basis for processing personal data.
             . Explicit CONSENT from users before collecting data.
             . Right to access: users can request all data held about them.
             . Right to erasure ("right to be forgotten"):
               users can request deletion of their data.
             . Data breach notification within 72 HOURS.
             . Data Protection Officer (DPO) required for some organizations.
             . Data transfers outside EU require adequate protections.

       FINES:
             . Up to 4% of global annual revenue OR EUR 20 million.
             . Meta fined EUR 1.2 billion in 2023 for GDPR violation.




    2. HIPAA — HEALTH INSURANCE PORTABILITY AND ACCOUNTABILITY ACT (USA):
             . US law protecting patient health information (PHI).
             . Applies to healthcare organizations and their vendors.
             . Cloud provider must sign a Business Associate Agreement (BAA).
             . AWS, Azure, GCP all offer HIPAA-compliant services (with BAA).
             . PHI must be encrypted at rest and in transit.
             . Access logs required for all PHI access.



    3. PCI DSS — PAYMENT CARD INDUSTRY DATA SECURITY STANDARD:
             . Required for ANY organization storing, processing, or
               transmitting credit/debit card data.
             . 12 requirements covering: network security, encryption,
               access control, monitoring, testing, security policy.
             . eSewa and Khalti processing card payments must comply.
             . Annual assessment by Qualified Security Assessor (QSA).

    4. ISO 27001:
             . International standard for Information Security Management.
             . Certifiable: third party audits and certifies compliance.
             . Proves to customers that your security is systematic.
             . Cloud providers hold ISO 27001 certification.

    5. NEPAL — NRB DIRECTIVES:
             . Nepal Rastra Bank requires financial institutions to:
               . Keep core banking data within Nepal for critical systems.
               . Conduct annual security audits.
               . Report security incidents within defined timeframes.
               . Use encrypted communication for all financial transactions.
             . Digital Nepal Framework 2019 defines cloud policy for government.






  ....................................
  B. DATA RESIDENCY AND SOVEREIGNTY
  ....................................

    . DATA RESIDENCY: requirement that data must be stored within
      a specific geographic region.
      "Customer data of Nepal citizens must remain in Nepal."




    . DATA SOVEREIGNTY: data is subject to the laws of the country
      where it is stored.
            US data stored on AWS us-east-1 → subject to US laws.
            US government can compel AWS to hand over that data.
            EU organization's data in US → may violate GDPR.




    . SOLUTIONS:
            . Use cloud regions in your home country.
              AWS has regions in Mumbai (closest to Nepal currently).
              Some countries (India, UAE, Saudi Arabia) require
              specific data to stay within their borders.
            . Private cloud: data never leaves your own data center.
            . Contractual protections with cloud providers.




    . DATA EXAMPLE — Where major cloud providers have regions:
      .....................................................
            Provider   Nearest to Nepal    Compliant For
            .....................................................
            AWS        Mumbai, India       Most South Asian data
            Azure      Pune/Mumbai, India  Most South Asian data
            GCP        Mumbai, India       Most South Asian data
            .....................................................
            No cloud provider has a datacenter IN Nepal currently.
            Nepal government data requiring local storage must use
            private cloud or own data centers.

7.8  DATA PRIVACY AND COMPLIANCE
------------------------------------------------------------

  . DATA PRIVACY = protecting individual's personal information
    from unauthorized collection, use, or disclosure.

  . PERSONAL DATA INCLUDES:
          Name, email, phone number, address, ID number,
          location data, IP address, cookie data, biometrics,
          financial data, health records.




  . PRIVACY PRINCIPLES (based on GDPR and global standards):
          1. PURPOSE LIMITATION: collect data for SPECIFIC stated purposes.
             Do not use birth data collected for health forms
             to send marketing emails.
          2. DATA MINIMIZATION: collect ONLY what is necessary.
             If you only need email, don't collect phone and address too.
          3. STORAGE LIMITATION: don't keep data longer than needed.
             Delete customer data when they close account.
          4. ACCURACY: keep data accurate and up to date.
          5. INTEGRITY AND CONFIDENTIALITY: protect data with appropriate security.




  ....................................
  A. PRIVACY BY DESIGN
  ....................................

    . Build privacy protections INTO the system FROM THE START.
    . Not an afterthought, not a bolt-on.

    . 7 principles by Ann Cavoukian (Ontario Privacy Commissioner):
                  1. Proactive, not reactive
                  2. Privacy as default setting
                  3. Privacy embedded into design
                  4. Full functionality (positive-sum not zero-sum)
                  5. End-to-end lifecycle security
                  6. Visibility and transparency
                  7. Respect for user privacy


  ....................................
  B. DATA CLASSIFICATION
  ....................................

    . Classify data by sensitivity → apply appropriate controls.

          .......................................................
          Classification    Examples              Controls Required
          .......................................................
          Public            Marketing materials   No restriction
          Internal          Employee handbook     Access control
          Confidential      Business strategies   Encryption + Access
          Restricted        Medical records,      Encryption + MFA
                            Financial data,       + Audit logs +
                            Passwords, Keys       Regulatory compliance
          .......................................................




    . Example for a hospital cloud system:
            .....................................................
            Data                 Classification   Encryption  Audit
            .....................................................
            Hospital logo        Public           No          No
            Staff directory      Internal         No          No
            Employee salaries    Confidential     Yes         Yes
            Patient diagnoses    Restricted       Yes         Yes (all access)
            HIV test results     Restricted       Yes         Yes (every query)
            .....................................................


  ....................................
  C. BREACH NOTIFICATION
  ....................................

    . WHEN a breach occurs (and breaches do occur),
      laws require NOTIFICATION within specific timeframes.

                .......................................................
                Regulation   Notification To       Time Limit
                .......................................................
                GDPR         Data Protection Auth  72 hours
                HIPAA        HHS + affected users  60 days
                PCI DSS      Card brands + banks   Immediately
                NRB Nepal    Nepal Rastra Bank     Immediately
                .......................................................


    . BREACH RESPONSE PLAN (must have BEFORE a breach):
            1. Detection: identify that a breach has occurred.
            2. Containment: stop ongoing breach, isolate systems.
            3. Assessment: what data was exposed? How many affected?
            4. Notification: inform regulators, affected individuals.
            5. Remediation: fix the vulnerability, restore systems.
            6. Post-incident review: how to prevent recurrence?

7.9  GOVERNANCE MODELS
------------------------------------------------------------

  . CLOUD GOVERNANCE = the framework of policies, processes,
          and standards that control how cloud is used, managed,
          and secured across an organization.



  . WHY GOVERNANCE IS NEEDED:
          . Without governance: every team does what they want.
            Random cloud services, inconsistent security, budget chaos.
          . With governance: consistent security, controlled costs,
            compliance maintained, accountability clear.






  ....................................
  A. CLOUD GOVERNANCE FRAMEWORK COMPONENTS
  ....................................
      
          1. POLICIES:
                   . Rules that MUST be followed.
                   . "All data stored in cloud must be encrypted."
                   . "All cloud accounts must have MFA enabled."
                   . "No public S3 buckets without CISO approval."
      
          2. STANDARDS:
                   . Specific technical requirements that implement policies.
                   . "AES-256 encryption must be used for all data at rest."
                   . "TLS 1.2 minimum for all data in transit."
      
          3. PROCEDURES:
                   . Step-by-step instructions for implementing standards.
                   . How to create a new cloud account (approved process).
                   . How to onboard a new cloud vendor (security review checklist).
      
          4. GUIDELINES:
                   . Recommended best practices (not mandatory).
                   . "Consider using Reserved Instances for cost savings."
      
          5. CONTROLS:
                   . Technical enforcement of policies.
                   . AWS Service Control Policies (SCPs): prevent certain
                     actions across entire AWS organization.
                   . Example SCP: "Block any attempt to disable CloudTrail."
                     Even if a compromised admin tries to turn off logging,
                     the SCP prevents it.




  ....................................
  B. CLOUD CENTER OF EXCELLENCE (CCoE)
  ....................................

    . A centralized team that governs cloud usage across
      the entire organization.

    . RESPONSIBILITIES:
            . Define cloud standards and best practices.
            . Review and approve cloud architecture designs.
            . Manage cloud costs and optimization.
            . Ensure security and compliance.
            . Train development teams on cloud best practices.
            . Evaluate new cloud services before adoption.

    . TEAM COMPOSITION:
            . Cloud architects
            . Security engineers
            . Finance/FinOps specialists
            . DevOps engineers
            . Legal/compliance representation


  ....................................
  C. CSPM — CLOUD SECURITY POSTURE MANAGEMENT
  ....................................

    . Automated tools that continuously scan cloud configurations
      for security misconfigurations and compliance violations.

    . CHECKS PERFORMED:
            . S3 buckets publicly accessible? → Flag and alert.
            . Security groups with 0.0.0.0/0 SSH? → Flag.
            . RDS databases publicly accessible? → Flag.
            . MFA not enabled for root account? → Critical flag.
            . Encryption not enabled for EBS volumes? → Flag.



    . TOOLS: AWS Security Hub, Prisma Cloud, Wiz, Lacework.



    . DATA EXAMPLE — CSPM audit report for hypothetical org:
            ......................................................
            Check                        Status     Risk Level
            ......................................................
            MFA on root account          FAIL       Critical
            CloudTrail enabled all reg.  FAIL       High
            Public S3 buckets (3 found)  FAIL       High
            Encrypted EBS volumes        PASS       -
            Security group 22 open       FAIL       High
            Unused IAM access keys       FAIL       Medium
            VPC Flow Logs enabled        PASS       -
            GuardDuty enabled            PASS       -
            ......................................................
      Total: 4 FAILURES needing immediate remediation.




  ....................................
  D. GOVERNANCE FRAMEWORKS — INDUSTRY STANDARDS
  ....................................

    . NIST CYBERSECURITY FRAMEWORK (CSF):
            Five functions: IDENTIFY, PROTECT, DETECT, RESPOND, RECOVER.
            Widely adopted framework for cybersecurity governance.
            Used by governments and large enterprises globally.

    . CIS CONTROLS (Center for Internet Security):
            18 prioritized security controls.
            Implementation Groups 1, 2, 3 based on organization size.
            Start with IG1 (basic hygiene) before advanced controls.

    . COBIT (Control Objectives for Information Technologies):
            IT governance framework.
            Aligns IT goals with business objectives.
            Used for internal audits and governance reporting.

    . ISO 27001 / 27002:
            International standard for information security management.
            Certifiable — shows external parties you're serious.
            Required by: European contracts, financial institutions.

7.10  SECURITY CASE STUDIES
------------------------------------------------------------

  ....................................
  CASE STUDY 1: CAPITOL ONE DATA BREACH (2019)
  ....................................

    . One of the largest financial data breaches in US history.
    . 100 MILLION customers affected.
    . Cost: USD 80 million regulatory fine + USD 190 million
      class action settlement.

    . WHAT HAPPENED:
            . Capital One hosted data in AWS (IaaS).
            . Web Application Firewall (WAF) was misconfigured.
            . The WAF had overly permissive server-side request forgery
              (SSRF) protection.
            . A former AWS employee (Paige Thompson) discovered the
              misconfigured WAF.
            . Used SSRF to query AWS instance metadata service.
            . Retrieved temporary AWS credentials for a role
              with excessive S3 read permissions.
            . Downloaded 100 million customer records from S3.

    . ROOT CAUSE ANALYSIS:
            .....................................................
            Factor                    Details
            .....................................................
            Misconfiguration          WAF allowed SSRF to metadata
            Excess permissions        IAM role had too much S3 access
            No anomaly detection      Large download not flagged
            No principle of least     Role could read all S3 buckets
            privilege
            .....................................................



    . LESSONS LEARNED:
            . WAF rules must be rigorously tested and regularly audited.
            . IAM roles must follow least privilege (only needed buckets).
            . IMDSv2 (metadata service v2) prevents SSRF attacks.
            . Anomaly detection must alert on large data downloads.
            . Encryption at rest would not have prevented this —
              the attacker had VALID AWS credentials (IAM issue, not crypto).









  ....................................
  CASE STUDY 2: MICROSOFT EXCHANGE SERVER + CLOUD MISCONFIGURATION (2023)
  ....................................

    . Chinese hackers accessed email accounts of US government
      officials and European organizations.
    . Compromised: US Secretary of State's emails, US Department
      of Commerce.
    . Method: forged authentication tokens using stolen
      Microsoft cryptographic signing key.
    . A single stolen key allowed access to Microsoft 365
      email accounts of any organization.

    . CLOUD GOVERNANCE FAILURE:
            . Microsoft's audit logs did NOT capture the token forging
              in a way that customers could detect in their own tenants.
            . Customers had no visibility into this attack.
            . Violated the shared responsibility: Microsoft must protect
              its own signing keys — they failed their responsibility.

    . LESSONS:
            . Cloud providers are not immune to breaches.
            . Key management is critical at provider level.
            . Security visibility must extend to cloud provider activities.









  ....................................
  CASE STUDY 3: TESLA — KUBERNETES CRYPTOJACKING (2018)
  ....................................

    . Tesla's Kubernetes management dashboard (Kubernetes Dashboard)
      was exposed to the internet WITHOUT AUTHENTICATION.

    . WHAT HAPPENED:
            . Attacker found the unsecured dashboard via internet scan.
            . Logged in (no password required — default config).
            . Deployed a cryptocurrency mining container inside
              Tesla's Kubernetes cluster.
            . Mining container throttled CPU to avoid detection.
            . AWS billing for compute would skyrocket (Tesla's cost).
            . Attacker gets free computing power (Tesla pays).

    . ROOT CAUSE: MISCONFIGURATION (most common cloud issue!)
            . Default Kubernetes Dashboard deployment has no auth.
            . Nobody checked: is this exposed to the internet?

    . LESSONS:
            . Default configurations are often INSECURE.
            . Never expose management interfaces to public internet.
            . Enable authentication on ALL management tools.
            . Monitor cloud costs — sudden spike = possible compromise.
            . Use network policies to restrict Kubernetes dashboard access.
            . Never leave default configurations in production.









  ....................................
  CASE STUDY 4: SMALL BUSINESS — RANSOMWARE IN CLOUD (2024)
  ....................................

    . Hypothetical Nepali logistics company (100 employees).
    . Moved all operations to cloud (AWS + Google Workspace).
    . No security training, basic IAM setup, no MFA.

      
      
          . INCIDENT TIMELINE:
                  Day 0: Phishing email sent to finance employee.
                  Day 0: Employee clicks link, enters credentials on fake
                         Google login page. Credentials stolen.
                  Day 1: Attacker logs into Google Workspace.
                         Reads 6 months of emails, financial plans.
                  Day 3: Attacker uses credentials to access AWS console
                         (same password — no MFA).
                  Day 4: Attacker downloads all S3 data (customer records,
                         contracts, financial data — 2 TB total).
                  Day 4: Attacker deploys ransomware across all EC2 instances.
                         All servers encrypted. All backups deleted.
                  Day 5: Company discovers attack. All systems offline.
                         Ransom demand: USD 50,000 in Bitcoin.
      
          . DAMAGE ASSESSMENT:
                  .....................................................
                  Impact               Value
                  .....................................................
                  Downtime             7 days
                  Revenue loss         Rs 35 lakhs
                  Recovery cost        Rs 20 lakhs
                  Ransom paid          USD 50,000 (Rs 65 lakhs)
                  Customer trust       Severely damaged
                  Regulatory fine      Under investigation
                  Total estimated      Rs 1.5 crore+
                  .....................................................
      
          . WHAT COULD HAVE PREVENTED THIS:
                  . MFA on all accounts → stolen password useless.
                  . Separate password for AWS → no lateral movement.
                  . S3 versioning + backup to separate account → ransomware fails.
                  . Employee security training → phishing detected.
                  . Incident response plan → faster recovery.
                  . Immutable backups → restore without paying ransom.
                  . Cost: Security setup = Rs 50,000/year.
                    Incident cost: Rs 1.5 crore. ROI = 30x.
